# ITM-1267 The review page loads the modules of the route it shows, in one wave

**REGISTER**

## Outcome

Why it is so today: ITM-1116 (92fb59aea) made the page name its views in `data-views` and the frame import each of them at
start (`src/site-frame/dashboard-app.mjs:53-60`), so that the frame names no view and registers every view's
strategies at load (`src/site-frame/register.mjs`: "so that a job any route of the page prepares finds every recipe").
Then 66afb81ff measured 15 levels of modules on the review page found one after the other, and added the generated
block of preloads (`src/site-frame/preloads.mjs`, TST-1290003), which names the static closure of the page's script and
of every view in `data-views`. The block is right about what the page loads; what the page loads is every view.

Measured at the head with `closure` of `preloads.mjs` (sizes of the files, gzipped each):

| What | Modules | Bytes | Gzipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| everything `docs/index.html` loads today, for every route | 279 | 3.50 MB | 1,107 KB |
| the frame alone (`dashboard-app.mjs` and its imports) | 196 | 1.93 MB | 641 KB |
| beyond the frame: review-pages | 18 | 199 KB | 66 KB |
| trace-pages | 9 | 115 KB | 37 KB |
| implementation-pages | 7 | 162 KB | 50 KB |
| test-pages | 7 | 101 KB | 33 KB |
| maintenance-pages (with `src/mail-routes/vendor/`) | 29 | 651 KB | 182 KB |
| settings-pages | 34 | 876 KB | 241 KB |

So `#uc` or `#plan` loads the mail libraries and the settings forms, which they never show.

After the change, as MOD-site-frame says: a view's `routes` are stand-ins — `name`, `entry`, `entryShown`, `title` and
`module` —, and its `strategies` stay in its view module, so that the frame still registers every strategy at start;
each route's own file is imported only when the route is first shown, embedded (`embedRoute`, `#spec`'s specification,
`#module`'s component diagram) or asked for its subject. `preloads.mjs` writes, beside the block, for each route module
the modules and files it loads beyond the block, as data in the page; the frame adds those as `modulepreload` and
`preload` links at once before it imports the route, so the route's modules arrive in one wave, not level by level
(66afb81ff's reason stands). TST-1290003 becomes: each page preloads its frame and its views' stand-ins, and names
each route's own modules as data.

Design principles (AGENTS.md §2 *Designing*): ch. 10 *plug-in architecture* — "The plug-in is only loaded into main
memory when you actually click the button"; the frame stays the plug-in manager and names no view (§2.4: a new view
changes nothing of the frame). §2.2 — the lists of modules are generated by `preloads.mjs` from the imports, never kept
by hand. KISS — no bundler and no build step; the page's text still holds everything it loads.

Not part of this item: the frame's own closure, 196 modules and 641 KB gzipped on every page, the main page included,
of which `src/repository-hosts/` is 241 KB, `src/markdown-render/` 150 KB and `src/work-plans/` 107 KB. What of it a
route needs is a question of its own, after this item has measured what the stand-ins leave.

## The reading of the accepted text

Two sentences of MOD-site-frame hold together.
- "A route that names `module` is drawn from the route that `module` gives: its `name`, `entry`, `entryShown` and
  `title` stand in the view module, and the frame imports the rest when the route is first shown, embedded or asked for
  its subject" (MOD-site-frame.md:181-185).
- `Route` has `render` as a member every route has; `subject`, `leave`, `unsaved` and `module` are optional
  (MOD-site-frame.md:155-162).

Sprint team1/120's stop ("ITM-1267's stop", `docs/backlog/sprints/team1/120.md`) found 204 test files failing with a
stand-in that has no `render`. Those tests render a route of `view.routes`, which uses `Route` as accepted. So the reading
must keep `render`:
- Each stand-in carries `render`, and `subject` where its route has one. Each imports the route's file through `module`,
  once, and calls the route's own. No function of MOD-site-frame writes the stand-ins, and no view's Uses change.
- `leave` and `unsaved` are given by the route the frame shows, which it has imported by then. A stand-in gives neither,
  and "a route that gives none holds none" (:162).
- Sprint 120's stop read these delegations, one in each of the six views, as one rule kept six times. The architecture
  states the stand-in once, at :181-185; each view writing its own stand-ins is that one rule followed, not a second
  copy of it.

Before selection, the planning stand-in shows whether this reading answers the type: with it, the tests that render a
route of `view.routes` keep passing. The time of its CI job is named against the two-minute limit (`A PULL REQUEST'S CI
RUNS WITHIN TWO MINUTES`): the patch of sprint 120 took `tests/site-frame-preloads.test.mjs` from 2.5 s to 28 s alone.
